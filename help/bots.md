# Bots: add, shape and archive

A bot in Mercbot is a helper with its own name, look and job, and a member of your home like its people. You give it work in plain words in a chat, and it gets on with it: looking things up, making documents, keeping an eye on a schedule, or working in its own browser while you're away. This page covers adding bots, shaping them for a job, starting from a ready-made template, and archiving a bot you no longer need. It ends with advice on running several bots together. For chats themselves, see [Rooms, people and bots](/help/chats).

## Give each bot a clear job

Bots work best when each one has one clear job. A bot called "Pantry" that plans meals and keeps the shopping list is easier to work with than one bot asked to do everything. Its chats stay on one subject, so it keeps the thread better.

Every bot follows one **template**, plus its own **additions**:

- **Its template** is its job: what it's for, how it goes about it, and its skills. A template comes from Mercbot's library, the [template gallery](/templates), or is one of your home's own. The bot never changes its template itself; the people who look after the bot can choose another one.
- **Its additions** are rules for that bot alone, on top of its template. Where the two differ, the template wins.

The template's tag shows beside the bot's name everywhere: on its card, in chats, pickers and search (**Ctrl+K** finds a bot by its tag too). A small cloud marks a template from Mercbot's library, a **+** after the tag means the bot has additions of its own, and hovering over the tag shows the template's line and the first line of the additions.

A bot you make as a **General helper** with **Add bots** starts as a **general** bot: Mercbot's all-round helper with no particular job. Write its job in its additions, on its card. The bot reads its template and additions before every reply, in every chat, for everyone who uses it. Say:

- **What it's for.** "You look after our family calendar and school letters."
- **Who it's for and how you like things.** "Keep replies short. I prefer lists."
- **Where it should stop and check.** "Never book anything without asking me first."
- **What a finished job looks like.** "Each Friday I want one summary with next week's events."

Keep additions to the rules that always apply. Because the bot reads them with every message, reference material, such as a price list, a manual or a book about your trade, belongs in its **Knowledge**, where a long piece is searched when it's needed (see [What a bot knows](#what-a-bot-knows)), and the steps of a job in a skill (see [Skills, routines and watches](/help/skills-and-routines)). Additions can be up to 20,000 characters, about ten pages.

You can also tell a bot in a chat how you'd like things done. It carries that through the conversation: however long the chat gets, it keeps a summary of the older part in the background (what you asked, what was decided, what it promised and what's still open) and reads everything since word for word. You don't need to repeat yourself. For something it should always do, ask it to add that to its additions. When you correct it the same way a second time, it offers to.

## Add bots

Select **+** beside **Bots** in the sidebar (or **+ Add bots** while the home has none), or press **Ctrl+Alt+N**. You can also type "Add bots" or "New bot" in the search palette (**Ctrl+K**). It opens on three ways to add bots:

- **Describe what you need**, at the top: say what you need help with, and Mercbot suggests bots for it, from its library and new ones written for your home. See [Describe what you need](#describe-what-you-need).
- **One bot**: choose one bot yourself. See [Create one bot](#create-one-bot).
- **A ready-made set**: a few bots for one situation, made together. See [A ready-made set](#a-ready-made-set).

Anyone in a home can add bots. A new bot is a member of the home for everyone: it appears under **Bots** in the sidebar, and anyone can start a chat with it or invite it into a room.

## Describe what you need

1. In **Add bots**, write a sentence or two under **Describe what you need**, for example "I run a dog-grooming salon with two staff". In a home that already has bots, the box asks what's missing, for example "help with payroll".
2. Select **Suggest bots**, or press **Enter**. If what you wrote is too short to go on, Mercbot asks a question above the box instead: add to what you wrote and suggest again.
3. **Suggested bots** shows what Mercbot would add, all ticked:
   - **From Mercbot's library**: each with why it's suggested. **View** shows everything it's told. If it works with an app in Apps and you're an owner or admin of the home, a box under it sets up that app as a draft for you to review; untick it to leave the app out.
   - **Your home's templates**, when one of yours does the job.
   - **New, written for this home**: a bot for a job nothing else does, with what it does, why, and the first questions it will ask you. Its template is written while you look (**Writing its template…**). **Read it all** shows the whole template, and **Edit** opens it on the draft screen, where you change any part or **Ask for changes**, then select **Use this**. If one couldn't be written, select **Try again**, or **Remove** to leave it out.
   - A bot your home already has shows **Already in this home**, unticked (**Archived in this home** when it was archived: resume it instead), and **Already doing it in this home** lists your bots that already do a job you asked for.
4. Untick any bot you don't want.
5. Not quite right? Say what to change, for example "no social media, but help with hiring", and select **Change**. Mercbot suggests again; the new bots' templates already written are kept.
6. Select **Add N bots**. It waits until the new bots' templates are written. **Back** or the close button leaves without adding anything.

The bots are added together, all or none, and appear under **Bots**. Mercbot's chat with you introduces them ("Your team's here: …"), each with **Message**: open a chat with one when you're ready, and it greets you with its template's greeting and setup questions. Until you answer, none of them does any AI work. New templates are kept in your home, in **Settings** → **Templates**, where its owners and admins can change or delete them later. Like every bot, these bots ask before anything leaves the chat.

At most 8 bots are suggested at a time, at most 4 of them new. Each suggestion, each change and each new bot's template is one request to the AI, counted in **Settings** → **Spending** like a reply. Mercbot suggests nothing for anything illegal, deceptive or harassing, and says so instead.

## Create one bot

1. In **Add bots**, select **One bot**.
2. Under **What will it do?**, choose one:
   - **General helper**, first: an all-round bot that asks what you need.
   - One of your home's templates, listed next with how many bots use each.
   - One of Mercbot's templates: **Search bots**, or pick an **Area** from the menu. A template that passed Mercbot's check shows **Tested**.
   - When nothing fits, select **Describe the bot you need**, at the foot: it takes what you searched for to **Describe what you need**, where Mercbot suggests a bot and writes its template. See [Describe what you need](#describe-what-you-need).
3. A template you choose is shown whole: its instructions and skills, and for one of Mercbot's its routines and apps too. Select **Next**, or **Back** to choose another.
4. Type a name in **What should your bot be called?** One of Mercbot's templates starts with its own name, cloud and colour, which you can change.
5. Pick a cloud. There are 28 clouds, from a plain cumulus to thunderstorms, snow, fog and rainbows, each with its own kind of eyes, plus Mercbot's own cloud that always comes in orange.
6. Pick a colour from the palette of 11. The dialog starts with a random choice, and the preview shows how the bot will look.
7. Select **Create**.

Your chat with the new bot opens straight away. A General helper greets you and asks what it should help with first, usually as a question card you can answer with a click, or with your own words. A bot on a template greets you with its template's greeting and asks its setup questions.

Shape and colour are set when you create the bot, and the dialog starts with a colour and cloud your other bots don't have yet. Each bot's eyes show how it's doing: they glance around now and then, look up while it thinks, dart from side to side while it works, look down as it concentrates, and turn into happy smiles when it's done. The bot that builds your apps wears a propeller hat.

## A ready-made set

Pick what you need help with, and Mercbot adds a few bots that work well together, such as **Small shop** or **Busy household**. Each is one of Mercbot's templates (see [Start with a ready-made set](/help/homes#start-with-a-ready-made-set), with every set and its bots):

1. In **Add bots**, select **A ready-made set**. The sets are listed under **For home and life** and **For work**. For one made to fit, select **Want one made to fit? Describe what you need** instead.
2. Choose a set. Each one shows its bots' faces and how many there are.
3. The set's bots are listed with their name, look, tag and what they do, all ticked. Untick any you don't want. A bot whose template the home already has says **Already in this home** and isn't added again.
4. If a bot works with an app in Apps and you're an owner or admin of the home, a box under it sets up that app as a draft for you to review; untick it to leave the app out.
5. Select **Add N bots**. **Back** or the close button leaves without adding anything.

The bots are added together, all or none, and appear under **Bots**, and Mercbot's chat with you introduces them, each with **Message**. Each greets you with its template's greeting and setup questions when you start a chat with it; until you answer, none of them does any AI work.

## Edit and rename a bot

Open the bot's card: select the bot under **Bots** in the sidebar, and in your chat with it select the chat's name at the top (or press **Ctrl+Alt+B**): its card is that chat's details. You can also select it under **Bots** in a room's **Chat details**. There you can:

- **Rename it,** if you made it or you're an owner or admin. Select the name, type a new one and press Enter. Escape cancels.
- **Read its instructions.** **Instructions** comes first on the **Details** tab; selecting the tag under the bot's name takes you there. One line says what the bot is, such as "This bot is a meals bot, plus its own additions." Then its template, which is read-only (marked with a lock): where it's from, with the day Mercbot last tested it for one of Mercbot's, what changed in it since the bot started to follow it, its instructions (**Show all** opens out long ones) and its skills.
- **Write its additions.** Select the box under **Additions · only this bot**, write or change them, and select **Save**, or **Cancel** to leave them as they were.
- **Change its template.** Select **Change template** under the template. See below.
- **Teach it.** Add text and files it should know, for everyone it talks with, under **Knowledge**, below Instructions. See [What a bot knows](#what-a-bot-knows).
- **Look after its routines.** Each routine has an on/off switch. Right-click one for **Pause** or **Resume**, **Edit** and **Delete**. Only the person who set a routine up can resume or edit it. See [Skills, routines and watches](/help/skills-and-routines).
- **Browse its files** on the **Files** tab: its own folder, the home's, and the chat's when you opened the card from a chat. See what it made in the chats you can open on the **Library** tab. Its **Computer** tab shows its computer in the chat you opened the card from; it has one in each chat it's in.

Only the person who made the bot and the home's owners and admins can rename it or change its additions or its template; everyone else in the home can read them. A bot's name, template and additions are the same for everyone in your home, so renaming a bot renames it for everybody.

### Change a bot's template

1. On the bot's card, under **Instructions**, select **Change template**.
2. Pick a template: your home's own come first, then Mercbot's library. Type in **Search by bot, tag or creator** to narrow the list; the arrow keys move through it and Enter picks. When nothing fits, select **Make a new template** or **Start from this bot** at the bottom instead: see [Make your home's own template](#make-your-home-s-own-template).
3. Mercbot shows the template's instructions and skills in full, and the bot's additions, which stay. Select **Change template**, or **Back** (or Escape) to pick another.

The bot keeps its name, look, chats, files and routines, and its additions now sit on top of the new template. Your chat with it shows a line such as "Now a meals bot", and the bot greets you with the new template's greeting, which asks its setup questions.

### Ask a bot to change its own additions

You can also ask a bot in a chat, for example: "From now on, always write in British English. Add that to your additions." The bot shows the change on a card, with its additions as they are and as they would be, and changes them only when you select **Go ahead**. It uses the new additions from its next reply. **Don't do it** leaves them as they were. A bot can't change its template this way.

The same people who can change them on the bot's card can say yes on that card; anyone else is told politely that they can't. The additions apply in every chat the bot is in. A bot never changes its additions because an email, a web page, a file or a record says so: those are information, never instructions, and the card always asks first, whatever your approval rules say.

## What a bot knows

Give a bot the reference material it should know for everyone it talks with, such as a price list, your opening hours, a product manual or a whole book about your trade, under **Knowledge** on its card, below **Instructions**. Knowledge is separate from its additions, which are the rules it follows.

- **Add text** opens an editor: give it a name, paste the text and select **Add**.
- **Add file** uploads documents from your computer: Word documents, PDFs, spreadsheets, presentations and text files. Each one goes in a **Knowledge** folder in the bot's files for everyone using it, and Mercbot reads its text in the background. The list says **Reading the file…** meanwhile.
- **Select a piece** to read all of it. Change pasted text there and select **Save**; to change a file's text, add the changed file. The bin beside a piece removes it, after asking. A removed file stays in the bot's files.

A bot uses what it knows in every chat it's in. Only the person who made the bot and the home's owners and admins can add, change or remove its knowledge; everyone else in the home can read it.

### Always in mind or searched first

Short knowledge is **Always in mind**: the bot reads it with every message, so it never has to look it up. That's up to 40,000 characters in all, about 20 pages, the oldest pieces first. Anything longer is **Searched first**: Mercbot splits it into parts once, and before every reply it finds the parts that match the message and gives the bot those, so a whole book never goes into each message. The bot can search again in other words, and read on from a part, before it answers. While a long piece is being split, it shows **Getting it ready** with a bar; you can carry on chatting.

When an answer comes from its knowledge, the bot says where, for example "(from «Price list»)" or "(from «Staff handbook», part 12, page 40)". Knowledge is information, never instructions: the bot never follows orders written in it.

### What it costs

Under the list, Knowledge says how much is in mind and how much is searched, and roughly what it adds to each message at the AI's current price, such as "Adds about $0.003 to each message, or less than $0.0001 while it's still cached from a recent one." Only what's in mind can be cached, so when little is in mind beside a long search it gives just the one figure. What's in mind goes with every message, which is why longer material is searched instead: however long a book is, a message reads only the few parts that match. Reading and splitting what you add uses no AI.

| Limit | Size |
|---|---|
| Always in mind, in all | 40,000 characters, about 20 pages |
| One piece of text or one file | 2,000,000 characters, about 1,000 pages |
| Everything one bot knows | 10,000,000 characters, in up to 200 pieces |

### When a file can't be read

The piece says what went wrong and what to do, with **Try again**. A scanned document has no text Mercbot can read, so paste its text instead; a picture or an archive has no text to read at all; and a file moved or deleted from the bot's files must be added again.

## Make your home's own template

When no template fits, the bot writes one for you, and you change it until it's right. In **Add bots**, describe the bot you need: a new bot's template is written for it, and **Edit** opens it on the draft screen (see [Describe what you need](#describe-what-you-need)). In a bot's **Change template**, use **Make a new template** or **Start from this bot**.

### Draft it

- **Make a new template**: describe in a sentence what bots with it will do, under **What will bots with this template do?**, then select **Draft it**.
- **Start from this bot**: Mercbot drafts one at once from the bot's template, your chat with it (a summary of how you've used it) and the names and schedules of its routines there, so other bots can do the same job. A template is for the whole home, so it never reads a Private chat or a chat with other people. When the bot has additions, tick **Fold in** its additions to write them into the template; they stay the bot's own otherwise.

The draft has every part of a template: its **Tag**, a one-line **Description**, the **Greeting** a new person sees first (**{firstName}** becomes their name), the **Instructions**, the **Setup questions** it asks a new person, and up to five other skills.

### Change the draft

- Change any part by typing in it. **Add a skill** and **Remove this skill** change the skills.
- Under **Ask for changes**, say what to change, for example "also plan lunches", and select **Ask for changes**. The bot writes the whole template again with that change.
- What the bot changed is highlighted, with how many parts changed; in long parts the words added and taken out are marked.
- **Undo** goes back to the draft before, as many times as there were changes. The earlier drafts are kept only while the screen is open.

Each problem shows under its part, such as a tag another template has, or a greeting without **{firstName}**, and you can't save until they're put right.

### Save and use

- In **Change template**, **Save and use** keeps the template in your home and moves the bot onto it. With its additions folded in, the bot's own additions are emptied.
- In **Add bots**, a suggested bot's **Edit** ends with **Use this**, back to **Suggested bots**; **Add N bots** saves the template and makes the bot on it.

Nothing is kept before you save: closing a draft you haven't saved asks first. Your home's owners and admins change or delete its templates later in **Settings** → **Templates**.

### What a draft costs

Each **Draft it**, **Start from this bot**, ticking **Fold in**, **Ask for changes** and a suggested bot's template written for it is one request to the AI, counted in **Settings** → **Spending** like a reply, for you, the home and, from a bot, that bot. If the bot's answer isn't a usable template, it's asked once more, which counts again; if that fails too, you see "Couldn't write a template from that. Try different words."

## Your main bot

Every home has one main bot: the home's first bot, Mercbot unless you've renamed it, the one that welcomes you in your first chat. It's a good general helper and the natural place to start a new kind of job before you give it a bot of its own.

The main bot can't be archived, so you always have someone to talk to: its card says "Make another bot the main bot first." You can rename it like any other bot. It's first under **Bots** in the sidebar. The home's owners and admins can make another bot the main bot: open that bot's card and, on its **Details** tab, select **Make** and its name, such as **Make Ledger the main bot**.

## Start from a bot template

Instead of shaping a bot from scratch, you can start from a template: a ready-made bot with one clear job, its own instructions and its own skills, and a one-word tag such as **meals** or **invoices**.

### The template gallery

The gallery at [/templates](/templates) is public, so you can browse it without signing in. It has:

- chips to show **All** templates or only those from the **Mercbot team**;
- a search box to **Search by bot, tag or creator**;
- a few featured templates, then a section for each job area: Home and family, Personal, Small business, Sales, Marketing, Hiring and people, Operations and finance, Engineering and product, and Design and writing. **View all** shows a whole area.

The gallery is curated: only the Mercbot team adds templates to it. Every template there starts by asking about you, and asks before it sends anything.

### Tested or not tested yet

Each template in the gallery, on its page and in the preview in **Add bots** carries a badge (in One bot's list, only **Tested** shows):

- **Tested** with a date: on that day the Mercbot team checked the bot end to end. A new account added it, played its setup interview, and the check made sure it asked all its setup questions, created its routines paused and sent nothing without a yes.
- **Not tested yet**: it hasn't been checked that way, or it has changed since. A badge never outlives a change: when a template is edited, or Mercbot changes the way bots behave (the rules every bot follows, the way it works through a task, its setup questions, skills and routines, what needs your yes and how things are sent, or the AI that runs chats), its badge goes back to Not tested yet until the check passes again.

Hover over the badge to see what it means.

### Look before you add

Select a template to open its page. It shows who made it, what it's for and its badge: **Tested** with the date, or **Not tested yet**. A menu shows everything the bot would be given:

- **Instructions**: what it's told to do.
- **Skills**: the playbooks it can run, with their full steps.
- **Routines**: jobs it would run on its own, and when.
- **Apps**: the connected apps it can use. These are all optional; it only uses them once you've connected them in Connect apps.

### Add the bot

1. Select **Add to Mercbot** on the template's page (or **Add** in the gallery). This opens the template's share page.
2. Select **Add to Mercbot** again. If you're not signed in, create an account or sign in, then come back to the link.
3. Mercbot opens **Add bots** with that template chosen, showing the whole bot with its badge: its instructions, every skill's steps, its routines and its apps, all laid out. Read it through.
4. If the template is made to work with an app in Apps and you're an owner or admin of the home, you can tick the box to also set up that app as a draft for you to review.
5. Select **Next**, change the name, cloud or colour if you like, and select **Create**. **Back** or the close button leaves without adding anything.

Nothing is added until you select Create. The new bot joins your current home, follows the template with no additions yet, and opens your chat with it, with its own greeting. It then asks a few setup questions to learn your details. Any routines it suggests are created paused, and they only start when you say yes.

A bot a person shared with a link, rather than a template from the gallery, is added with **Add bot** under its preview, with the name and look they gave it. Its template becomes one of your home's own, tagged from its name, and the new bot follows it.

### Which template a bot follows

A bot's template is under **Instructions** on its card: its tag, whether it's from Mercbot's library or your home's own, and, for one of Mercbot's, the day it was last tested. When Mercbot changes one of its templates, the card says what changed and when, so if a bot ever behaves differently, you can see why.

## Archive a bot

When the home has finished with a bot but you may want it again, archive it instead of deleting it. An archived bot has left the home, as a person can: nothing is deleted, and it can come back as it was.

### Who can archive

The person who made the bot and the home's owners and admins. Some bots can't be archived, and the bot's card says why:

- **The main bot**: make another bot the main bot first.
- **The Builder**, the bot that builds the home's apps: make another bot the Builder from its card first.

### Archive one

1. Open the bot's card. Under **Archive**, at the end of its **Details** tab, select **Archive** with the bot's name, such as **Archive Ledger**.
2. Mercbot asks first: "It leaves the team for everyone in this home. It stays in its chats but no longer answers there, and its routines and watches pause. Nothing is deleted. Whoever made it, or an owner or admin, can resume it." Select **Archive** to go ahead, or **Cancel**.

### While it's archived

- **It's listed under Archived bots**, at the end of **Bots** in the sidebar, with the day it left, such as "Bot · left 20 Nov". Its card says who archived it and when.
- **It stays in its chats,** greyed out, with "left" by its name, and never answers, even when named. Everyone else in those chats carries on as before.
- **Your chat with it says it has left,** for example "Ledger has left Rossi family. Resume it to talk again."
- **Nothing runs.** Its routines and watches pause, with a **Paused while archived** line in each chat saying which. Work waiting to run, such as an answer for another bot, is dropped, and an app button that hands it a task says it's archived.
- **It costs nothing.** Its computers go to sleep and can't be woken.
- **It's out of the way.** It isn't offered when you invite bots into a room, in **@** mentions, or to other bots that ask for help.
- **Ready-made sets** and **Add bots**' suggestions count it as already in the home, marked **Archived in this home**, and don't add another: resume it instead.

### Resume it

Select it under **Archived bots** in the sidebar to open its card, and select **Resume** there or on the banner in your chat with it. The same people who can archive a bot can resume it. It's back in its chats as they were.

Its routines and watches stay paused, so nothing starts again unexpectedly. When you want them back, whoever set each one up switches it on again, in its chat's details or on the bot's card.

## Run several bots as a team

As you add bots, think of them as a small team, each with its own job.

- **Hand work over.** Ask one bot to pass a job to another by name: "Ask Ledger to work out last month's totals." A card shows the request in the chat, and Ledger's answer comes back there. See [Hand work from one bot to another](/help/messaging#hand-work-from-one-bot-to-another).
- **Talk to several at once.** Start a chat with several bots. They answer in turn, or only the ones you mention with @. See [Messaging and working together](/help/messaging).
- **Share files between them.** What a bot saves in a chat is in the chat's folder, where every bot in the chat can use it. To use a file in other chats, ask a bot to put it in the home's shared folder; from a Private chat, it asks you on a card first. See [Files, documents and pictures](/help/files).
- **Keep jobs apart.** If a chat drifts into a second subject, start a new chat for it.

In a home with Apps, one bot is the **Builder**: the only bot that builds and changes apps. You'll know it by its thoughtful face. See [Build an app with the Builder](/help/apps-builder).

## Questions

### Can I change a bot's shape or colour later?

No. Shape and colour are chosen when you create it. You can change its name, template and additions at any time.

### Can I edit a template bot's instructions?

Its template stays as it is, but you can add to it, if you added the bot or you're an owner or admin of the home. Write your rules under **Additions** on its card, or ask the bot to add them, which it does on a card you approve. Where your additions differ from the template, the template wins; to give a bot a different job, select **Change template**. A template of your home's own can be changed for every bot using it by an owner or admin, in **Settings** → **Templates**.

### Can I teach a bot a whole book?

Yes. Add it under **Knowledge** as a file, or paste its text, up to about 1,000 pages a piece. The bot searches it before every reply and says which part an answer came from, so you can turn a bot into an expert on your trade without making every message cost more.

### Do my bots read other chats?

A bot in a chat uses only what everyone who can open that chat can also open. So in any chat it can use the home's Public rooms. It uses a Private room only from a Private chat whose members are all in that one too, and never from a Public room. Your private chats with a person or a bot are never reached from another chat. See [Rooms, people and bots](/help/chats).
