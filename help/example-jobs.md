# Example jobs for a bot

Not sure what to hand over first? The short scenarios below show the kind of work a Mercbot bot can take on, for a family, a small business or a company. Each one describes a role, what the bot does day to day, and what it needs from you. Use them as a starting point and change the details to fit your own life or work.

## The family organiser

The Okafors have three children, two calendars and a fridge door covered in school letters. Their bot reads the school emails in the shared family inbox, picks out dates, kit lists and payment deadlines, and adds events to the family calendar. It asks first before inviting anyone to an event. Every Sunday evening a routine posts a short summary of the week ahead: who needs to be where, and what to pack.

**What it needs:** the family's mail and calendar, connected in **Connect apps** for **The whole home**, so both parents' chats can use them.

## The meal planner

A bot plans five dinners a week around everyone's tastes, the nights with football practice, and one child's nut allergy, which it remembers for good. It writes a single shopping list grouped by aisle. When asked, it searches a supermarket's website for the items and builds the basket, then stops before checkout so a person can check it and pay.

**What it needs:** to be told once about diets and allergies, and a login for the shop if it should fill the basket.

## The household paperwork keeper

A bot keeps an eye on bills, renewals and warranties. It searches the mailbox for invoices, puts the amounts and due dates in a spreadsheet, and flags anything that has gone up since last year. A month before the car insurance renews, it compares a few quotes on the web and reports back. It never pays or switches anything itself.

## The shop's stock keeper

A small gift shop asks the Builder for a stock app: products, quantities and suppliers. Its everyday bot is asked to watch the stock table. When something drops below its reorder level, the bot drafts an order email to the right supplier and shows it in the chat. The owner edits the draft if needed and presses **Send**.

## The enquiries desk

A plumber gets enquiries through a public contact form, built as an app. A bot reads each new enquiry, sorts it by urgency, checks the calendar for free slots, and drafts a reply offering two times. Submissions from the public are treated as untrusted, so the bot works from them as data and a person approves each reply before it goes.

## The sales call preparer

Before each call on a salesperson's calendar, a bot researches the company on the web, reads the last emails with that contact, and writes a one-page brief: who they are, what was promised, and three good questions to ask. A routine runs each morning at 8 for that day's calls, and the brief arrives in the chat.

## The hiring coordinator

A small team is hiring a designer. Their bot keeps the candidates in a hiring app, drafts polite replies to applicants, suggests interview slots from the interviewers' calendars, and writes up each interview from the notes people paste in. Anything sent to a candidate waits for a person's **Go ahead**.

## The engineering helper

A software team connects GitHub. Each morning a bot posts a digest of open pull requests: which are waiting for review, which have failing checks, and which have gone quiet. When a build breaks, someone asks the bot to read the failing checks and summarise the likely cause. It comments on GitHub only when asked, and asks first.

## The weekly numbers report

An operations manager asks a bot to put together the same report every Friday: figures from a few spreadsheets in the company's drive, compared with last week and turned into a short slide deck with a chart. The bot keeps the deck's layout the same each week, so it's quick to read.

## Turning a role into a lasting bot

A good scenario becomes a reliable bot when its job is clear and stays clear. To set one up:

1. **Give it one job.** Create a separate bot for each role with **New bot**, and give it a name that says what it does. A bot that does one thing well is easier to trust than one that does everything.
2. **Tell it the specifics once.** Explain who's involved, which accounts to use, what to avoid and how you like results presented. Lasting facts are remembered across chats.
3. **Run the job by hand first.** Ask for it a few times in the chat and correct anything that's off, until the result is what you want.
4. **Save the method.** Ask the bot to save what it did as a skill, or teach it by recording yourself doing the task on its computer.
5. **Then make it repeat.** Ask for a routine, such as "every Friday at 4". The bot tells you when it will run, with your time zone, and you can pause or edit it in the bot's **Details** panel.
6. **Set your limits.** In **Settings**, add rules for what the bot may do on its own and what it should always ask about first.

You don't have to start from scratch. The **Bot templates** gallery at [/templates](/templates) has ready-made bots, each with one clear job, made by the Mercbot team for families, small businesses and companies. Each template starts by asking about you, shows you its full instructions, skills and routines before you add it, creates its routines paused until you say yes, and asks before it sends anything.
